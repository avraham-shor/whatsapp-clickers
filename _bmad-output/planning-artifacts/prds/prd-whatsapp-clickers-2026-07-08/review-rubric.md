# PRD Quality Review — WhatsApp Clickers

Reviewed: `prd.md` + `addendum.md` (2026-07-08), against the PRD Quality Rubric. Calibration: internal/family-events pilot, chain-top (feeds architecture → epics → stories); pilot-scope deferrals (self-serve purchase, payments, export, marketing site) are explicitly documented and are not penalized.

## Overall verdict

This is a genuinely decision-ready pilot PRD: it states its one big bet (WhatsApp as a full Play Channel, superseding two prior documents, decision logged), gives it a kill criterion (SM-5 plus the addendum's "if refuted… re-weigh" branch), and tags every unconfirmed inference with an owner. What's at risk is concentrated in exactly the place stories will land first: the flagship WhatsApp Channel ships against a conversation UX that does not exist yet (OQ-7, not marked blocking), and a handful of cross-channel behaviors — who counts as a "WhatsApp-Channel Participant" mid-question, Speed Bonus ties, AI grading in flight at Reveal — are left for engineers to guess. None of this is fatal; all of it should be resolved before §4.2 story creation.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Decisions are stated as decisions, with dates and audit pointers. §0 declares precedence outright ("Where this PRD contradicts those documents, **this PRD wins**") and names the major override; the addendum's "Overrides of prior documents" section gives downstream readers the audit trail. Trade-offs name what was given up, not just what was chosen: FR-4's dynamic channel assignment explicitly trades away the opt-in step ("avoids double-sending and caps message costs; no explicit channel opt-in step"), and the addendum's unofficial-gateway note is an honest anti-recommendation with the failure mode spelled out ("risk number bans mid-event — a catastrophic live-event failure mode"). The Open Questions are actually open — OQ-2 has three options in the addendum with "none selected yet," and each OQ carries an owner. `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at real tensions (Speed Bonus fairness at FR-8; "load-bearing for the business model" at §6.2).

### Findings
- **medium** OQ-7 aggregates the flagship feature's missing UX without a blocking marker (§8 OQ-7, §4.2) — OQ-1 is explicitly annotated "Blocking for architecture, not for this PRD," but OQ-7 — which contains the entire WhatsApp conversation design ("how time limits are communicated without a visible timer, Reveal parity… handling of non-answerers, winner-moment parity for UJ-2") — carries no equivalent sequencing statement, even though FR-4–FR-7 stories cannot be written until it is resolved. *Fix:* split OQ-7 (WhatsApp conversation design vs. dashboard deltas vs. Spectator screen) and mark the conversation-design item as blocking for §4.2 epics/story creation, the way OQ-1 is marked for architecture.

## Substance over theater — strong

No furniture found. There are no persona bios at all — §2.1 uses Jobs To Be Done, and persona-like characters exist only as named UJ protagonists who each carry a real scenario (Duvid in UJ-5 exists specifically to make the kosher-smartphone bet concrete, down to the edge case). NFRs are product-specific numbers, not boilerplate: "3s lobby-count latency," "5 seconds to 95% of WhatsApp-Channel Participants," "P×Q×3 outbound messages" — and each unconfirmed number is tagged `[ASSUMPTION]` rather than asserted. The Vision could not be swapped into another PRD: "a participant with a kosher smartphone — WhatsApp but no browser — can play the whole game" is this product and no other. The counter-metrics (SM-C1 AI leniency, SM-C2 message thrift) are the opposite of theater — they name the specific ways the team would be tempted to cheat its own metrics.

The only unearned-sounding claim is §1's "that no local competitor offers" (three-stage validation) — asserted without a pointer to where Discovery established it. Low impact for a pilot; worth sourcing before the commercial-phase PRD reuses it.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The PRD has a thesis and bets on it: the Haredi family-event wedge, reached through WhatsApp-native join, with the kosher-smartphone hypothesis as the moat (§1). The feature set follows the thesis rather than a backlog — the WhatsApp Play Channel is the thesis made into requirements, and the addendum states the falsification branch explicitly ("If refuted… its cost/complexity should be re-weighed before the commercial phase"). Success Metrics validate the thesis, not activity: SM-5 tests the kosher-phone hypothesis directly, SM-6 exists precisely because "pilot-phase manual provisioning otherwise hides builder-usability problems" — a metric designed against the pilot's own blind spot. Qualitative-first pilot success (SM-1/SM-2) is a stated decision ("decision logged 2026-07-08"), not an evasion. MVP scope kind is coherent: an experience-proving pilot that keeps the full game loop and defers everything monetization-shaped, with each deferral logged.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

The best FRs here are exemplary — FR-1's idempotent re-JOIN, FR-7's shared server-side cutoff ("the earlier of the Question's timer expiry or the Organizer's explicit close action"), FR-16's stage-ordering consequences are all directly testable, and weasel words are nearly absent from the document. But the standard is uneven: 7 of 18 FRs (FR-3, FR-8, FR-10, FR-14, FR-15, FR-17, FR-18) have no Consequences block. For most, the FR statement itself carries the acceptance criteria (FR-8's "the first submission received wins" is testable as written; FR-10 legitimately defers to EXPERIENCE.md's component behavior tables). The real gaps are in scoring and cross-channel edges — exactly where story creation will lean hardest.

### Findings
- **medium** "WhatsApp-Channel Participant" is delivery-defined but used for intake and results (§4.2 FR-4 vs. FR-5/FR-6; Glossary "Plays through exactly one Play Channel at a time") — FR-4 assigns the channel dynamically per Question by web-connection state, but FR-5 ("A WhatsApp-Channel Participant can answer") and FR-6 ("each WhatsApp-Channel Participant who answered receives their grade") reuse the term without saying whether it means "assigned WhatsApp at question open" or "answered via WhatsApp." A web-assigned Participant who texts "ב" mid-question: is that an answer (FR-8 implies yes — "first submission received wins… on the other channel") or unrecognized text getting FR-2's help reply? And do they then receive the FR-6 result message? An engineer can build three different systems from this. *Fix:* state that FR-4 assignment governs delivery only; intake (FR-5) accepts answers on either channel from any Participant; and define FR-6 result routing (e.g., result goes to the channel the answer arrived on).
- **medium** FR-17 has no testable consequences and Speed Bonus edge cases are undefined (§4.6 FR-17) — "the system awards them automatically based on answer receipt timestamps" leaves open: tie-breaking when two correct answers share a timestamp (likely at pilot scale with second-granularity WhatsApp timestamps), default point/bonus values ("default value TBD" appears only for time limits, FR-11), and whether a Fuzzy- or AI-graded Free-Text answer earns the same points as Exact. Cross-channel timestamp skew is honestly parked at OQ-2, but within-channel ties are not. *Fix:* add a Consequences block: tie rule (e.g., stable order by receipt sequence), defaults, and stage-independent scoring stated explicitly.
- **low** FR-16 is internally tense about AI grading in flight at Reveal (§4.5 FR-16) — the consequence "All grading for a Question completes before the Organizer's Reveal is published" implies Reveal blocks on grading, while the adjacent assumption says "fail-closed to non-AI grading rather than blocking the Reveal." The uncovered case is an AI call still pending (not timed out, not errored) for a last-second answer when the Organizer hits Reveal. *Fix:* one sentence — pending-at-Reveal is treated as AI-unavailable and degrades to two-stage grading, or Reveal waits up to N seconds.
- **low** FR-18's "on all channels" is precise only at game end (§4.6 FR-18) — the parenthetical covers the winner moment ("web winner takeover; WhatsApp final results message"), but whether WhatsApp players get anything Leaderboard-shaped between Questions beyond FR-6's personal rank is unstated. OQ-7 lists "Reveal parity" and "winner-moment parity" but not between-question Leaderboard parity. *Fix:* either state "WhatsApp Channel: personal rank only (FR-6), no full Leaderboard push" or add it to OQ-7's enumeration.
- **low** WhatsApp Free-Text answer constraints have no web parity (§4.2 FR-5 vs. §4.3 FR-10) — the web gets a 200-char limit and explicit submit; a WhatsApp Free-Text reply has no stated length bound, and a voice note or image sent during an open Free-Text Question falls ambiguously between FR-5 intake and FR-2's "media messages get the generic help reply" assumption. *Fix:* state the WhatsApp Free-Text rule (e.g., text only, truncated/rejected past 200 chars; media during an open Question gets the help reply and is not an answer).

## Scope honesty — strong

The scope machinery does real work. §5 Non-Goals excludes things a reader would plausibly assume ("there is no human operator behind the number"); §6.2 pairs each deferral with its consequence, including the self-aware "`[NOTE FOR PM]` This is load-bearing for the business model"; the filter-certification line correctly splits the product constraint (in scope) from the bureaucratic approval (not a software deliverable). Fifteen inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags, seven owned OQs, and three `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts is high open-items density — appropriate for pilot stakes, and the blocking one (OQ-1) says who it blocks. Overrides of the brief and EXPERIENCE.md are done in the open with dates, not silently.

### Findings
- **medium** Privacy/data handling is a silent omission (whole document) — the platform's identity primitive is the phone number (Glossary: "identified by their WhatsApp phone number"), the Organizer sees a live participant list (FR-13), and §2.1 makes gatekeeper trust a stated job ("recommending or allowing it carries no risk") — yet nothing anywhere states retention, who can see phone numbers, or what happens to the data after a family's event. This is the one omission a reader must infer, and architecture needs at least a stance to design storage. *Fix:* add a short pilot-level data-handling requirement or an owned OQ-8 (retention window, phone-number visibility on the dashboard, post-event deletion), even if the answer is "keep everything during pilot, decide before commercial."

## Downstream usability — strong

This PRD is chain-top and built like it. The Glossary opens with "Downstream workflows must use these terms exactly" and the FRs largely obey; IDs are contiguous and unique (FR-1–18, UJ-1–5, SM-1–6 + SM-C1/C2, OQ-1–7); every SM names the FRs it validates; every FR names the UJs it realizes; the Assumptions Index round-trips (see Mechanical notes for two trivialities). All external references resolve on disk: `briefs/brief-whatsapp-clickers-2026-06-18/brief.md`, `ux-designs/ux-whatsapp-clickers-2026-07-05/DESIGN.md` and `EXPERIENCE.md`, and `.decision-log.md` all exist, and the addendum's overrides section gives auditors the pointers. Sections extract cleanly — §4.2 can be handed to an epic on its own. The one term that wobbles under extraction ("WhatsApp-Channel Participant") is filed under Done-ness above, since it is a behavior-definition problem, not a traceability one.

## Shape fit — strong

Right shape for the product: consumer-facing live experience with meaningful UX → UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing and present; chain-top → traceability is heavy, correctly. The PRD resists two temptations well: it does not duplicate the final UX docs (§4.3: "this PRD adds no deltas"), and it does not pretend the pilot is a launch — §0 frames Chanukah 2026 as "the horizon but not the first milestone." Five UJs for four user-facing flows plus the new channel is proportionate, not UJ density for its own sake. No over- or under-formalization found.
